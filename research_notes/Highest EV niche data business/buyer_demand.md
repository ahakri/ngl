## What do buyers actually pay for niche datasets?

### Takeaway
Institutional investors pay tens of thousands to low hundreds of thousands of dollars per dataset per year, with most subscriptions tracked by Neudata in the $25k-$150k band and a long tail of elite datasets in the millions; self-serve marketplace listings for corporate/SMB buyers start at $25-$600 per month. The published "average" figures conflict badly ($80k vs $1.1m vs $1.6m per dataset) because they measure different things, so the $25k-$150k bracket is the most usable planning number for a small vendor.

### Cited Findings

**Hedge funds / asset managers**
- Neudata found datasets are more likely to fall within the lower price brackets of $25k up to $150k per year; over 40% of ESG dataset subscriptions it tracked cost less than $25,000 a year; about 30% of transactional-data subscriptions cost more than $150,000 a year. (Article date not confirmed in snippet; believed to be older than 2025, flag as such.) — [Institutional Investor, "How much are managers paying for data"](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsx5x6m2pew8jzgavmyo/corner-office/how-much-are-managers-paying-for-data)
- A Neudata survey of 60 buyers (reported Sept 2024, older figure) found the average buyer works with 20 data vendors and spends $1.6m annually, which Hedgeweek converts to about $80,000 per dataset (a derived average). Large multi-strategy hedge funds average $5m per year across 43 vendors (about $116k per vendor, my arithmetic). — [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/hedge-funds-gear-up-for-a-2025-alternative-dataset-budget-boom/)
- Neudata's Feb 2025 press release puts the average dataset's revenue at $1.1 million per year, with some elite datasets earning over $20 million from investment clients alone. This is vendor revenue per dataset across all clients, not the price one buyer pays. — [Neudata press release](https://www.neudata.co/press-release/alternative-data-spending-by-investment-management-firms-could-top-15-4bn-in-2025-according-to-neudata-s-latest-report)
- Neudata's education page gives estimated average spend per dataset in 2024 as $1.6m, excluding free datasets (again a per-dataset total, not a per-buyer price). — [Neudata](https://www.neudata.co/education/how-big-is-the-alternative-data-market-for-investment-managers)
- The typical alternative dataset is used by around 20 investment clients, down from 25 in 2024. — [Neudata, State of the alt data market 2026](https://www.neudata.co/blog/state-of-the-alternative-data-market-2026)
- Eagle Alpha says datasets in its financial-markets category (flows, trading signals) sell "in a region of $40 to $80k". Page undated. — [Eagle Alpha](https://dsg.eaglealpha.com/financial-alternative-data/)
- Neudata notes vendors' headline prices are often negotiated down by buyers, "sometimes significantly". — [Neudata](https://www.neudata.co/education/how-big-is-the-alternative-data-market-for-investment-managers)
- Buyers subscribe to an average of 19 alternative datasets annually (Neudata, cited secondhand by a vendor blog; low confidence). — [Paradox Intelligence](https://www.paradoxintelligence.com/blog/best-alternative-data-platforms)
- Over two-thirds of the 107 private-fund respondents (PE, hedge, VC) in Lowenstein Sandler's survey (fielded Nov 9 - Dec 8 2025, published Feb 2026) reported alternative-data budgets above $1 million; 89% plan to increase alt-data budgets in 2026. — [Lowenstein Sandler press release](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/firm-news/lowenstein-releases-2025-alternative-data-report-highlighting-rapid-ai-integration-rising-budgets-and-evolving-governance-demands); [report PDF](https://www.lowenstein.com/media/jujd45bp/alt-data-report-2025_final.pdf)

**Corporate mid-market / SMB (self-serve marketplace list prices)**
- Datarade listings show "starts at" prices from about $25-$50 per month (product-pricing feed, phone-number dataset) through $49/month (several firmographic and product-review feeds) and $249-$599/month, up to $8,000-$10,000/month for mobility/location datasets; many listings are per-record instead (e.g. $0.15 per row, $0.02 per company). Snapshot figures, undated, often shown with discounts. — [Datarade pricing-data category](https://datarade.ai/data-categories/pricing-data/datasets); [Datarade mobility-data](https://datarade.ai/data-categories/mobility-data/datasets); [Datarade company-revenue data](https://datarade.ai/data-categories/company-revenue-data/datasets); [Datarade product data](https://datarade.ai/data-categories/product-data/datasets)

All are press-reported, not company-confirmed terms. — [Quartz](https://qz.com/ai-training-data-pricing-licensing-deals-market-052126); [Everything as Digital substack](https://everythingasdigital.substack.com/p/why-data-is-becoming-a-new-asset)
- A small specialised example: an aerial survey firm's existing 3D and LiDAR datasets sold for A$1.07M combined. — [OriginDataPartners deal dataset on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/OriginDataPartners/ai-data-licensing-deals)
- An AI data broker is quoted saying deals now run $1.1M-$1.3M (secondary blog source, low confidence). — [FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-the-data-wars-ai-data-broker-says-deals-now-run-1-1m-1-3m/)

### Inferences
- A realistic price for a first niche dataset sold to funds by an unknown solo vendor is at the bottom of the bracket: roughly $15k-$50k per fund per year, given the $25k-$150k modal band, sub-$25k ESG pricing, and routine negotiation down from list. This is my inference; no source states a "new small vendor" price.
- With the typical dataset at about 20 investment clients, a typical dataset at $25k-$80k per client implies $0.5m-$1.6m revenue, which is consistent with Neudata's $1.1m-$1.6m per-dataset averages. That consistency suggests the three "conflicting" averages are different views of the same market.
- Self-serve marketplace buyers pay 10-100x less per customer ($300-$7,000 per year) than funds, so a solo founder needs hundreds of SMB customers to equal 5-10 fund contracts.

- No sourced prices for insurers, lenders, or real-estate investors buying niche third-party data.
- No sourced per-dataset prices for small AI-training data deals beyond the single A$1.07M example.

## Which dataset categories have rising demand in 2025-2026, and which are saturated?

### Takeaway
Web-scraped and transactional data remain the largest spend categories (web-scraped about 15% of spend, flat) and are the most crowded; buyers say they are looking for more B2B data, emerging-market data, and "under-the-radar" datasets, and AI has widened the buyer base by making raw data cheaper to clean. Hard, category-level growth rates for 2026 sit behind gated Neudata reports and were not obtainable.

### Cited Findings
- Web-scraped and transactional datasets continue to account for the largest share of alt-data spending (analysis of 2,805 datasets on Neudata's Scout platform; report published Feb 2026). — [Neudata](https://www.neudata.co/blog/state-of-the-alternative-data-market-2026); [PR Newswire](https://tools.prnewswire.com/en-us/live/20813/release/20260225EN94468)
- Web-scraping datasets were the largest category at 15% of total spend in 2025, roughly in line with 2024 (attribution uncertain between the two links). — [Alternative Fund Insight](https://alternativefundinsight.com/alt-data-spend-rises-17-in-2025-neudata/); [Neudata](https://www.neudata.co/blog/state-of-the-alternative-data-market-2026)
- Neudata says buyers are seeking more B2B data, data from emerging markets, transactional data, and under-the-radar datasets in 2025 (attribution uncertain; from trade coverage of Neudata's buyer survey). — [Alternative Fund Insight](https://alternativefundinsight.com/alternative-data-boom-set-to-continue/); [Hedgeweek](https://www.hedgeweek.com/hedge-fund-alt-data-spending-set-to-surge-says-new-research/)
- 97% of data buyers expect alt-data spend to increase or stay the same between 2026 and 2027, up from 89% in the 2025 survey; a three-year high (191 responses from providers and buyers; 60% of buyer respondents manage over $1bn). — [Neudata, Future of Alternative and Market Data 2026](https://www.neudata.co/blog/the-future-of-alternative-and-market-data-2026-budget-confidence-hits-a-3-year-high-while-ai-drives-operational-efficiencies)
- Alt-data use among private fund managers rose to 90% from 67% a year earlier (62% in 2023), with private equity showing significant increases. — [Lowenstein Sandler](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/firm-news/lowenstein-releases-2025-alternative-data-report-highlighting-rapid-ai-integration-rising-budgets-and-evolving-governance-demands); [PE Law Report](https://www.pelawreport.com/21440851/alternative-data-and-ai-becoming-integral-to-investment-processes-survey-finds.thtml)
- AI tools have made cleaning raw data cheaper, so managers now want direct raw feeds, and smaller managers who lacked technical resources are now buyers; Trustpilot is cited as a newer seller of review data. — [Business Insider via AOL, Feb 2026](https://www.aol.com/news/money-managers-hungrier-ever-obscure-140001573.html)
- Vendors adding AI features did not drive an above-expected uplift in buyer spending; 66% of buyers use AI/LLMs mainly for productivity and workflow efficiency. — [Neudata press release](https://www.neudata.co/press-release/ai-adoption-is-rising-but-alt-data-vendor-spend-hasn-t-neudata-report-finds)
- 44% of data providers offer access via Model Context Protocol (MCP), and over half of those charge no additional fee. — [Neudata](https://www.neudata.co/blog/the-future-of-alternative-and-market-data-2026-budget-confidence-hits-a-3-year-high-while-ai-drives-operational-efficiencies)
- Saturation signal: consumer-transaction data usage reportedly declined among Lowenstein respondents as firms became more selective (secondary source, no percentages, wording not confirmed against the report). — [Equity Data Science](https://equitydatascience.com/articles/alternative-data-portfolio-analytics/)
- Dilution signal: as the number of datasets grows, each is used by fewer firms (20 clients per dataset vs 25 in 2024). — [Business Insider via AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/money-managers-hungrier-ever-obscure-140001573.html)
- Eagle Alpha profiles more than 2,500 datasets across 16 categories and has added an AI-data vertical for AI companies. — [Eagle Alpha](https://www.eaglealpha.com/solutions/data-buyer-private-equity/); [Eagle Alpha, Nov 2026](https://www.eaglealpha.com/2026/11/11/digica-best-data-marketplaces-2026/)
- Older (pre-2022, flag as old): Eagle Alpha's Use Cases Edition 6 called labor-market data one of the most popular categories, with 74 labor-market, 63 geolocation and 40 real-estate datasets catalogued. — [Eagle Alpha PDF](https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/ea-pdf-items/Alternative+Data+Use+Cases_Edition6.pdf)
- AI-lab demand: of 52 AI data licensing deals Neudata tracked (to early 2025; post dated June 2025), news media made up 87% of textual deals and real-time retrieval was the use case in 77%; Neudata flags underrepresented languages and cultures as an opening for providers. — [Neudata, "Feeding the machines"](https://www.neudata.co/blog/ai-data-licensing-market-analysis)
- Private fund managers report tighter licensing terms and more restrictions on AI use of data, particularly model training. — [Lowenstein Sandler](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/firm-news/lowenstein-releases-2025-alternative-data-report-highlighting-rapid-ai-integration-rising-budgets-and-evolving-governance-demands)
- 94% of real-estate investors in Cotality's Q1 2026 survey consider AI-ready data critically or very important. — [Cotality](https://www.cotality.com/reports/beyond-the-baseline)

### Inferences
- Commoditised for a solo founder: broad consumer transaction panels, generic web-scraped e-commerce pricing, app/web traffic, geolocation. These are the biggest spend lines but dominated by scaled vendors (YipitData reportedly exploring a sale at $2.5bn-$3bn — [Pipeline Road](https://pipelineroad.com/news/20260821-carlyle-backed-yipitdata-explores-sale-at-2-5bn-3bn-valuatio)) and need proprietary panels.
- Web-scraped data being the largest category at a flat 15% share means the category is large but not growing in share; a new scraped dataset has to be a narrow vertical nobody covers, not a general one.
- The "raw feed + cheaper cleaning via AI" shift lowers the bar for a solo vendor: buyers increasingly accept less-polished niche data if the provenance and history are sound.
- "B2B data" and "under-the-radar datasets" being named by buyers is the best survey-level support for a niche B2B operational dataset built from public sources.

- Nothing found from BattleFin, Snowflake Marketplace, AWS Data Exchange, or the Bloomberg alt-data catalogue on fastest-growing categories or top-selling listings.
- No current Eagle Alpha "datasets in demand" ranking; only an old edition surfaced.

## What specific unmet needs or data gaps do buyers name?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced gap is private-company data: private-market GPs say they lack detailed debt, risk, and estimate data on targets and portfolio candidates. Beyond that, buyers ask for B2B, emerging-market and obscure datasets, and for delivery, tooling and support around data; evidence for insurance, lending and SMB gaps is older or vendor-sourced.

### Cited Findings
- S&P Global Market Intelligence's 2026 Private Equity and Venture Capital Report found many GPs unhappy with their access to detailed debt data and risk data on portfolio candidates, and flagged gaps in data on potential deal targets, especially third-party consensus estimates (via A-Team's summary; proportion not captured). — [A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/private-market-gps-unhappy-with-data-availability-unsure-about-ai-value/)
- Trade press reports that the private-markets boom is exposing data weak points. — [WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/node/7952911)
- Fewer than 10% of private equity and credit firms say they have achieved a high degree of data maturity (Crisil Coalition Greenwich, Q4 2025 study). — [Coalition Greenwich](https://coalitiongreenwichuat.crisil.com/press-release/private-equity-and-credit-firms-innovation-and-ai-adoption-slowed-siloed-systems-lack)
- Allvue's GP Outlook 2026 (102 interviews, North America and Europe): majority of GPs cite fragmented data, lack of expertise, limited integration and compliance worries. — [Business Wire, Feb 10 2026](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260210315493/en)
- Investors say even the best alt data is less valuable without the right delivery methods, analytical tools and hands-on support (Coalition Greenwich, Feb 2026). — [Coalition Greenwich](https://coalitiongreenwichuat.crisil.com/press-release/beyond-data-investors-ask-alt-data-providers-better-tools-and-support)
- Buyers seeking more B2B data, emerging-market data and under-the-radar datasets (Neudata buyer survey, 2025; attribution uncertain). — [Alternative Fund Insight](https://alternativefundinsight.com/alternative-data-boom-set-to-continue/)
- Barriers named in Neudata's 2025 survey: price negotiations, limited dataset trial conversions, and data-quality concerns. — [BigDATAwire / HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/this-just-in/neudata-survey-highlights-alternative-data-quality-concerns-and-budget-optimism/)
- Commercial property insurance (2024, older): 67% of commercial property insurance providers want a centralised data exchange covering property information, policy history and past claims; 64% said their appetite for data enrichment had grown in the past year (LexisNexis Risk Solutions study). — [FF News](https://ffnews.com/newsarticle/67-of-commercial-insurance-providers-want-property-data-exchange-appetite-for-data-has-grown-for-64-of-respondents)
- A 2026 ULI roundtable reported that owners, insurers, lenders and rating agencies rely on different property data points at different times and argued for standardised, verified property data. — [Urban Land](https://urbanland.uli.org/resilience-and-sustainability/how-better-property-data-can-improve-commercial-real-estate-insurance)
- Verisk describes an underwriting data gap for habitational (multifamily) property risks (vendor-sponsored content). — [Carrier Management](https://www.carriermanagement.com/perspectives/verisk/bridging-the-underwriting-data-gap-for-habitational-property-risks.htm)
- AI: underrepresented languages and cultural content named as a supply gap for model makers. — [Neudata](https://www.neudata.co/blog/ai-data-licensing-market-analysis)
- A broker site claims demand for legally cleared, AI-ready datasets is growing faster than supply (vendor claim, unverified). — [OriginDataPartners](https://huggingface.co/datasets/OriginDataPartners/ai-data-licensing-deals)

### Inferences
- Private-company data is the most consistently named gap, but the specific items GPs want (debt terms, risk data, consensus estimates) mostly require proprietary access. The solo-reachable slice is public-record proxies for private-company health: permits, licences, filings, hiring, pricing pages, government contracts, court/UCC records.
- Because PE alt-data adoption is rising fast but data maturity is low (<10%), PE buyers probably want finished, analyst-readable outputs, not raw feeds. That raises the product bar (dashboards, memos) but lowers the technical bar (no ticker mapping or point-in-time backtest).
- Insurance and lending gaps are real but lead to regulated, slow, enterprise buyers; they are poor first targets for a solo founder.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 buyer survey was found that lists named unmet categories with percentages (e.g. "X% want supply-chain data and cannot find it"). The Neudata reports likely contain this but are gated.
- No reliable sources found on unmet demand for SMB data, niche-industry pricing, supply-chain, labour-market, or government/regulatory datasets specifically; these remain hypotheses.
- No lender-side or SMB-credit data-gap survey found.

## What makes a small vendor's dataset purchasable, and how long are sales cycles?

### Takeaway
Funds reject most datasets they trial, chiefly over point-in-time integrity (timestamps showing when data was actually knowable, unrevised history) and missing or non-point-in-time entity/ticker mapping; provenance and licensing documentation is now scrutinised more because of AI-use terms. Evaluation-to-purchase typically takes three to six months, sometimes seven, and cycles remained long in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Quant funds reject most of the alt data they evaluate, often for reasons providers do not expect; trials frequently fail when timestamps do not show when a fund could actually have traded on the data. (Vendor blog.) — [Monda](https://www.monda.ai/blog/selling-data-to-hedge-funds-why-alternative-data-trials-fail-and-how-to-fix-them)
- Ticker changes, delistings and corporate actions cause hidden failures unless entity mapping is explicit and point-in-time. — [Monda](https://www.monda.ai/blog/selling-data-to-hedge-funds-why-alternative-data-trials-fail-and-how-to-fix-them)
- Backtesting against revised or restated history produces false signals; vendors are advised to supply verifiable point-in-time history. (Vendor source.) — [vBase](https://www.vbase.com/alternative-data/)
- A point-in-time feature joined with non-point-in-time mappings is no longer point-in-time. — [A-Team Insight](https://a-teaminsight.com/blog/how-much-hedge-fund-alpha-is-lost-before-the-model-even-runs/?brand=ati)
- When history is not point-in-time and mapping is not supplied, a short trial mostly measures how quickly the buyer can build a mapping. — [Precision Federal](https://precisionfederal.com/insights/evaluating-alternative-data)
- Buyer-side checks include asking for history as it existed on each date and testing entity matching with namesake companies to count wrong-company hits. — [Akta](https://akta.pro/blog/alternative-data-for-hedge-funds)
- "Tickerization" (mapping records to tradable securities) is described as essential enrichment for selling to investors. — [AltHub](https://althub.com/blog/data-enrichment-tickerization-explained/)
- Sales cycle: the validation phase can run three to six months while the fund tests the data before committing. — [Selling Signals podcast (Kai Searle episode)](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2567619/episodes/19540236-kai-searle-hiring-elite-data-sellers)
- Sales cycles "remain stubbornly long" despite market improvements (2026 episode covering trials, pricing, backtesting). — [Selling Signals podcast (Jordan Hauer episode)](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2567619/episodes/19335759-jordan-hauer-taking-stock-of-the-alternative-data-industry)
- Older figure (c. 2019-2020, attribution uncertain): alt-data sales can take up to seven months, and more than 75% of buyers need up to six months to test, evaluate and buy a dataset; onboarding new datasets takes considerable time. — [Open Quant Live Book, ch. 7](https://bookdown.org/souzatharsis/open-quant-live-book/the-market-the-players-and-the-rules.html)
- Limited trial conversion is named by Neudata as a market-wide barrier; "why trials fail to convert" is a topic of its 2026 report. — [BigDATAwire / HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/this-just-in/neudata-survey-highlights-alternative-data-quality-concerns-and-budget-optimism/); [Neudata](https://info.neudata.co/future-of-alternative-and-market-data-2026)
- Success selling to the largest funds does not carry over to the broader asset-manager market; the largest funds want raw data, others handle data differently. — [The Data Score](https://thedatascore.substack.com/p/why-some-data-companies-struggle); [Selling Signals podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2567619/episodes/19540236-kai-searle-hiring-elite-data-sellers)
- Compliance/provenance: Lowenstein's authors call for disciplined controls, auditable decisioning and a clear data-provenance strategy; 89% of respondents say providers are enabling AI interaction, producing tighter terms and more restrictions; nearly nine in ten firms have formal AI policies. — [Lowenstein Sandler](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/firm-news/lowenstein-releases-2025-alternative-data-report-highlighting-rapid-ai-integration-rising-budgets-and-evolving-governance-demands)
- Buyer concentration: Auren Hoffman has variously estimated about 100 funds (2019), 500-700 funds ingesting large amounts of data, and fewer than 100 funds spending millions on alt data; the figures conflict and span years. — [Hoffman thread, 2019](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1141010668985827328.html); [Hoffman substack](https://auren.substack.com/p/now-is-a-great-time-to-build-a-pure); [Hoffman substack](https://auren.substack.com/p/data-is-actually-not-a-great-vc-backed)
- DaaS Bible (2019, older): a niche can be dominated by one or two players, but dominance does not necessarily give pricing power. — [SafeGraph](https://safegraph.com/blog/data-as-a-service-bible-everything-you-wanted-to-know-about-running-daas-companies)
- Scouting platforms (Neudata, Eagle Alpha, BattleFin) are the standard discovery route for selling to funds. — [OriginDataPartners guide](https://origindatapartners.com/guides/sell-data-to-hedge-funds-platforms); [Neudata](https://www.neudata.co/blog/how-can-you-navigate-the-final-stages-of-institutional-data-licensing)

### Inferences
- Minimum viable fund-grade dataset for a solo founder: immutable, timestamped snapshots from day one (history cannot be backfilled honestly later), an explicit point-in-time entity-to-ticker map, a written provenance/collection-method note covering scraping legality and absence of PII/MNPI, and flat-file/cloud delivery. Starting collection early is the one thing money cannot buy later.
- With maybe 100-700 serious fund buyers and 3-7 month cycles, a solo founder should expect the first fund revenue no earlier than 6-9 months after having a multi-year history; fund sales are a second-stage channel, not the first one.
- Listing on a scout/marketplace substitutes for a sales team for discovery but does not shorten the trial.

### Gaps
- No sourced minimum history length (commonly cited as 3-5+ years) could be confirmed from a readable source.
- No sourced detail on standard DDQ contents, preferred delivery formats (S3/Snowflake/SFTP), or trial length; the relevant pages could not be fetched.
- Trial-to-purchase conversion rates are not available outside the gated Neudata report.

## Which buyer segments are fastest to sell to for a solo founder?

### Takeaway
No source ranks segments by speed of sale; the evidence only establishes that fund sales are slow (3-7 months, high trial rejection) and that self-serve marketplace buyers transact at $25-$600 per month list prices with free samples. The ranking below is therefore my inference, not a cited finding.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
Ranked by expected speed for a solo builder with no sales team (inference only):
1. **SMB / operators in a vertical niche, self-serve (card checkout, API or CSV).** Shortest cycle, no compliance review, $50-$600/month. Low revenue per customer; needs volume and SEO/community distribution.
2. **Mid-market corporate teams (sales, strategy, pricing, category) via self-serve or light-touch contract.** Purchases under a manager's discretionary limit avoid procurement. Corporates are a proven but harder-to-educate buyer.
3. **Small/emerging hedge funds and independent analysts.** Newly reachable thanks to cheaper data cleaning; less formal compliance than large funds, but still want a trial.
4. **Private equity deal and diligence teams.** Fast-growing adoption and budget, but want finished insight and often buy per-deal; relationship-led.
5. **Large hedge funds / quant funds.** Highest price ($25k-$150k+ each), but 3-7 month cycle, point-in-time history, ticker mapping, DDQ. Best treated as an upsell once 2-3 years of history exist.
6. **Insurers, lenders, AI labs.** Slowest or least reachable: regulated model governance (insurers/lenders) and deal flow concentrated in large rights-holders (AI labs).

The highest-EV shape consistent with this evidence: start collecting an immutable, timestamped, niche B2B/operational dataset from public sources now; monetise first via self-serve to operators and corporate teams in that vertical; list on Datarade/Neudata/Eagle Alpha; approach funds and PE once history and entity mapping are credible.
