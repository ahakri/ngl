# AI-era data demand: new data-business opportunities for a solo software founder (late 2026)

> Method note for the report writer. Research date 2026-11-27. This session's network blocked direct page fetches (indiehackers.com, beincrypto.com, dev.to all failed to resolve), so **every finding below comes from web-search result summaries, not from pages I read in full**. ## 1. AI visibility / GEO / AEO tracking: size, crowding, and niche angles

### Takeaway
This is the one AI-demand category with proven, fast, self-serve revenue (venture leaders at $10M+ ARR, at least one founder-reported bootstrapped tool at mid-five-figure MRR inside a year), but by late 2026 it is very crowded at every price point from $19/month to enterprise, incumbents (Semrush, Ahrefs, Yotpo, Writesonic) bundle it, and the obvious niches (local business, Shopify/e-commerce product recommendations) already have several bootstrapped entrants. A horizontal tracker is a poor bet; only a narrow vertical dataset or benchmark angle looks open.

**Data acquisition cost and method**
- Pay-per-use tracker on Apify: 5 AI engines for $0.30 per query; the same vendor puts DIY API builds at $0.30-$0.50 per validated datapoint (vendor claim) — [Apify actor](https://apify.com/doesaiknow/ai-brand-visibility-tracker-chatgpt-perplexity-gemini).
- Lowest published price cited is ZipTie at $0.01 per prompt per engine — reported in the search summary of a pricing roundup, most likely [Detailed](https://detailed.com/ai-visibility-tools-pricing/) (URL attribution not certain).
- API answers differ from what users see: Surfer reports only 24% brand overlap between API and scraped-interface results — [Surfer](https://surferseo.com/blog/llm-scraped-ai-answers-vs-api-results) (conflicted: Surfer sells interface-based tracking). Method explainers: [Metehan](https://metehan.ai/articles/how-ai-visibility-tools-collect-data), [Conductor](https://conductor.com/academy/scraping-vs-api), [seoClarity](https://www.seoclarity.net/blog/scraping-vs.-api).
- Answer variability is high; one source in the results calculates about 271 runs of one prompt for 90% confidence in a mention rate, and a SparkToro study is cited as finding under a 1-in-100 chance of identical brand lists across 100 asks. I could not pin either to a specific URL; candidates are [Spyglasses](https://spyglasses-dev.ngrok.io/blog/youre-wasting-money-on-prompt-tracking) and [Gracker](https://gracker.ai/white-papers/how-ai-prompts-actually-get-tracked). Treat as unverified.

### Inferences
- Rough polling budget: at $0.01-$0.30 per prompt-engine answer, a $200/month budget buys roughly 700 to 20,000 answers per month. That supports a narrow fixed panel (for example 200 prompts x 4 engines x weekly) but not statistically robust per-customer tracking at 100+ runs per prompt. A solo founder can afford a **published vertical benchmark**, not a per-brand tracker with honest confidence intervals. (My arithmetic from the prices above.)
- The strongest solo angle is not another dashboard but a fixed-panel dataset: "which products/vendors do the assistants recommend in category X, week by week", sold as a category report or feed to many buyers at once. One polling run serves every customer, so cost does not scale with customers, unlike per-brand trackers.
- Consolidation (Scrunch to Sitecore) and bundling by Semrush at $99 suggest horizontal tracking is commoditising; late entry there depends on distribution, not data.
- App/tool recommendation tracking (what coding assistants recommend as libraries, SaaS tools, APIs) did not appear in any result as an occupied niche, but absence from one search round is weak evidence.

### Gaps
- No audited revenue for any vendor; Profound ARR not found.
- Could not read the LLM Pulse interview (host blocked), so acquisition channel, costs and team size are unknown.
- No independent study comparing API versus interface accuracy.
- No evidence found on willingness to pay for category-level AI-recommendation benchmarks (as opposed to per-brand tracking).

## 2. Data for AI agents: what builders pay for, MCP and x402

### Takeaway
Agent builders pay real money for general web search/retrieval (Exa, Tavily), but that layer is venture-scale and capital-heavy. For small vertical data sellers the evidence is negative so far: MCP servers are overwhelmingly free, and x402 pay-per-call has high transaction counts but trivial dollar volume. Selling to agents is currently a distribution channel for an existing data asset, not a business in itself.

### Cited Findings
- Exa raised $250M Series C at a $2.2B valuation led by a16z, announced 20 May 2026 — [Exa blog](https://exa.ai/blog/announcing-series-c); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/exa-raises-250-million-for-ai-powered-search-infrastructure/).
- Sacra estimates Exa at about $100M annualised revenue in Sept 2026, up from $14M at end-2025, driven by coding agents and sales/research tools; this is an outside estimate — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/exa/); [Sacra research](https://sacra.com/research/100m-year-google-for-ai-agents/).
- Nebius agreed to acquire Tavily for $275M in Feb 2026, roughly 98x Tavily's 2025 revenue per Sacra (implying about $2.8M revenue); Tavily sells credit-based API access — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/tavily).
- Competitors include Parallel, Perplexity, You.com and Brave; per-request prices are falling — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/100m-year-google-for-ai-agents/).
- x402: 165M+ transactions as of May 2026 — [dev.to](https://dev.to/kirothebot/the-x402-revolution-165m-transactions-later-agent-to-agent-payments-are-real-1o0k) (developer blog). A figure of about 69,000 active agents and $50M cumulative volume by April 2026 is attributed to "Spark Research" secondhand in another developer post (exact URL uncertain; unverified).
- x402 dollar volume is tiny: 6.4 million payments tracked late July-August 2026 were worth less than $120,000 in total, and over 90% were under one cent — [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/state-of-ai-agent-payments-2026/).
- Typical x402 purchases are code review, financial intelligence, data and search APIs priced between a hundredth of a cent and a dime (Chainalysis, as reported) — [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/tiny-x402-payments-expose-gap-holding-ai-agents-back/); the same piece describes a bear case where wallets stay human-in-the-loop and payments remain niche.
- Example pricing: EmblemAI put 200+ trading tools behind x402 from $0.01 per market-data query (vendor) — [dev.to](https://dev.to/emblemai/x402-how-ai-agents-pay-for-api-calls-with-crypto-micropayments-3a5a).
- MCP: 11,000-15,000+ servers, under 5% monetised, "the vast majority are $0"; paid ones run $19-$149/month or about $0.009 per search — [dev.to](https://dev.to/t49qnsx7qtkpanks/15000-mcp-servers-97m-monthly-downloads-and-almost-no-one-getting-paid-4g7b); [dev.to](https://dev.to/whoffagents/pricing-an-mcp-server-in-2026-why-we-charge-19mo-when-the-market-average-is-0-nig). Authors are often building marketplaces (conflict of interest).
- One developer post is titled around a first commission of $0.21 from a monetised MCP server — [dev.to](https://dev.to/nicolas_fainstein/how-i-monetized-my-mcp-server-and-what-the-021-first-commission-means-3o1k).
- Marketplaces advertise 80-85% developer share (MCPize, Apify) — [dev.to](https://dev.to/namel/mcp-server-monetization-2026-1p2j) (marketplace-promotional). Apify's official terms: pay-per-event is the flagship model, 20% commission, platform usage costs deducted from payouts so profit can go negative; rental listings retired 1 Oct 2026 — [Apify docs](https://docs.apify.com/platform/actors/publishing/monetize); [Apify blog](https://blog.apify.com/standardizing-actor-pricing).
- Platform policy risk: guides report OpenAI's rules bar selling subscriptions/digital content through its app surface and Anthropic's docs do not describe paid listings — reported in MCP monetisation guides such as [Zuplo](https://zuplo.com/blog/mcp-monetization-models.md) (URL attribution not certain; unverified).

### Inferences
- Back-of-envelope: under $120K across 6.4M x402 payments in roughly five weeks, spread over all sellers, means a typical small data seller earns close to nothing from x402 today.
- The viable pattern is "own a fresh niche dataset, sell it by subscription to humans/companies, and additionally expose it via API/MCP". An MCP endpoint is a feature and a discovery surface, not the revenue line.
- Generic wrappers of free public sources get cloned immediately (see the tariff example in section 6, where six-plus Apify actors wrap the same USITC data), so scarcity must come from the dataset, not the interface.

### Gaps
- No evidence found of any small vertical data API with meaningful agent-originated revenue.
- No data on which vertical data agent builders most request but cannot get; I found no survey or marketplace demand ranking.
- Apify top-creator earnings for 2026 not found.
- Could not read the BeInCrypto or dev.to pages directly.

## 3. Training / eval data licensing to AI labs

### Takeaway
Lab spending is enormous but flows to (i) rights holders with large owned corpora and (ii) expert-labour and RL-environment vendors operating at $100M-$3B scale. I found no credible path for a solo founder who owns no corpus; brokers aggregate other people's rights, which needs partnerships and legal work the constraints exclude.

### Cited Findings
- Mercor: Sacra estimates $3B annualised gross revenue at end-Oct 2026, up from $760M at end-2025; H1 2026 gross revenue $614M — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/mercor/). Dealroom reports over $2B gross run-rate (July 2026 milestone) — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/137121-mercor-doubles-to-2b-gross-revenue-run-rate-as-ai-labs-buy-expert-data/). Revenue is concentrated in a few large labs — [The Information](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/a-competitor-of-scale-ai-doubles-its-revenue-thanks-to-reasoning) (paywalled; headline only). A report that Meta paused its contract after a March 2026 breach appeared in the search summary without a clear URL (unverified).
- Surge AI: RL environments marketed as its biggest product by Aug 2026; revenue about $1.2B in 2024 (Sacra estimate, relayed) — [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/report/surge-ai); [Enterprise DNA](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/ai-map/company/surge-ai).
- Micro1 claimed crossing $100M ARR — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/04/micro1-a-scale-ai-competitor-touts-crossing-100m-arr) (company claim).
- Anthropic leaders reportedly discussed spending over $1B on RL environments in a year — relayed in the search summary of Surge/Mercor coverage, likely [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/report/surge-ai) (attribution uncertain).
- Troveo expanded from video into audio, text, enterprise workflow, gaming and robotics data and says it has paid over $20M to content owners (company claim) — [IT Brief](https://itbrief.com.au/story/troveo-expands-ai-data-platform-into-five-categories).
- Protege listed among best-funded marketplaces with a $70M raise; segment concentrated in multimodal and consumer-contributed data — [Extruct](https://www.extruct.ai/data-room/ai-training-data-marketplaces/) (third-party analysis).
- Human Native: marketplace taking a cut of rights-holder deals plus transaction/monitoring fees — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/10/15/human-native-ai-startup-building-marketplace-for-data-hires-veteran-google-execs-top-ip-lawyer/); its CEO declined to say whether deals had been signed — [RightsTech](https://rightstech.substack.com/p/building-a-scale-model-for-ai-data).
- Headline deals: News Corp-Meta reportedly up to $50M/year for at least three years (March 2026); Taylor & Francis-Microsoft $10M initial (May 2024) — secondary compilation at [Origin Data Partners dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/OriginDataPartners/ai-data-licensing-deals); context in [Quartz](https://qz.com/ai-training-data-pricing-licensing-deals-market-052126).
- "Data pricing for AI is being negotiated without a stable model" — [IDC](https://www.idc.com/resource-center/blog/data-pricing-for-ai-is-being-negotiated-without-a-stable-model/); five pricing mechanisms from per-unit licensing to commissioning — [arXiv 2510.24990](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24990v1).

### Inferences
- Every observed deal involves either an owned archive or managed human experts. A solo software founder has neither.
- The only software-only wedge is small: building RL environments or evals as a contractor. That is services, sold through enterprise procurement to about five buyers, with slow sales cycles; it is not a data product and time to first revenue is unpredictable.
- A by-product opportunity exists: a dataset built for another purpose (for example a longitudinal panel of assistant answers, section 1) could later be licensed, but should not be the base case.

### Gaps
- No per-unit prices found for Protege, Troveo, Human Native, Defined.ai or Scale.
- No 2026 revenue figure found for Scale AI.
- No example found of a solo founder licensing a self-built dataset to a lab.

## 4. AI-infrastructure market data (GPU prices, data centres, power)

### Takeaway
Demand is real and prices are high ($9K/year per dataset is a public anchor), and there is a proven small-team precedent (Cleanview, Interconnection.fyi). But GPU price indices are now locked up by exchange partnerships (Ornn-ICE, Silicon Data-CME), and data-centre/power tracking already has focused specialists plus energy-data incumbents. This is the best category on price point; the open question is whether an unclaimed sub-niche remains.

### Cited Findings
- Ornn publishes the Ornn Compute Price Index (H100, H200, B200 spot); ICE and Ornn announced cash-settled GPU compute futures on 19 May 2026, pending regulatory approval — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/options/articles/ice-ornn-launch-gpu-compute-142609556.html); [Markets Media](https://www.marketsmedia.com/ice-ornn-to-offer-gpu-compute-futures/). Sources conflict on whether contracts had launched or were only announced.
- CME and Silicon Data announced (11 Aug 2026) H100 and B200 Rental Index Futures for trade date 5 Oct 2026, 730 GPU-hours per contract, financially settled, subject to review — reported in the search summary of [KuCoin blog](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/ai-compute-futures-cme-gpu) (secondary; launch not confirmed).
- Index quality is questioned because neither firm sees the closely held contracts between the largest players — [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/05/26/2026/the-future-of-ai-is-an-ai-futures-market).
- Volatility that creates demand for the data: Blackwell spot rental rose 48% from $2.75 to $4.08 per GPU-hour between mid-Feb and mid-April 2026 (Ornn index) — reported via [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com/b200-gpu-pricing-spot-market-model-releases/) and [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/ornn-exchange-gpu-rental-prices-rising/); H100 prices fell after an early-May spike — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/h100-rental-prices-slide-ornn-ai/). Ornn is venture-backed (Galaxy) — [Galaxy](https://galaxy.com/insights/perspectives/backing-ornn-the-financial-layer-for-compute).
- Cleanview public pricing: US data-centre dataset (every building, capacity, status, developer, tenant) $9,000/year; separate grid-power-projects dataset (behind-the-meter gas for data centres) $9,000/year; up to five users; API add-on $5,000/year; one-time download $4,900; free data-centre map as lead magnet — [Cleanview pricing](https://cleanview.co/pricing); [product page](https://cleanview.co/products/data-center-tracker).
- Interconnection.fyi describes itself as the only comprehensive real-time US interconnection queue database, includes data-centre projects by state and distribution-grid data, quote-based — [Interconnection.fyi](https://www.interconnection.fyi/dg/purchase-data) (self-description).
- Incumbents covering large-load/data-centre queues: Yes Energy Infrastructure Insights — [Yes Energy](https://yesenergy.com/new-build-dataset); Enverus — [Enverus](https://www.enverus.com/blog/queued-up-shifting-ground-for-large-loads/); Modo Energy — [Modo](https://modoenergy.com/research/large-load-interconnection-six-us-markets-data-centers); TeleGeography data-centre research — [TeleGeography](https://www2.telegeography.com/data-center-research-service); Blackridge US data-centre projects database — [Blackridge](https://www.blackridgeresearch.com/new-projects-near-me/data-center-facility-database/c/united-states/subscribe).
- Every RTO publishes queue data in a different format, which is why aggregators exist — [Build](https://build.inc/insights/interconnection-queue-data-center-developer-guide).

### Inferences
- Source data (RTO queues, utility large-load filings, permits, PUC dockets) is public and scrapeable, which fits the constraints; the value is normalisation, entity resolution and freshness. LLM extraction from filings is cheap and well within a low-hundreds monthly budget.
- Buyers (developers, utilities, infrastructure investors, equipment vendors, hedge funds) pay four to five figures per seat-year, so 10-20 customers make a solo business. Sales are outbound and B2B, so time to first revenue is likely months, not weeks.
- US whole-market coverage is taken. Remaining wedges would be narrower: utility large-load tariff and rate-case tracking, local permitting/moratorium/opposition tracking, non-US markets, or secondary GPU cloud list-price/availability across neoclouds for procurement teams (as opposed to financial indices).
- GPU price indices as a financial benchmark are closed to a solo founder (exchange relationships, transaction data access).

### Gaps
- No pricing found for datacenterHawk or Aterio (two searches returned unrelated "Atera" results); both are assumed quote-based but this is unconfirmed.
- Team size and revenue for Cleanview and Interconnection.fyi not found.
- No evidence gathered on which infrastructure sub-niches are unserved; the wedges above are my hypotheses.

## 5. LLM / API market data (model pricing and usage trackers)

### Takeaway

## 6. Other emerging data needs (tariffs/trade, energy, insurance, regulation)

### Takeaway
Tariff data is the clearest 2025-2026 shock-driven need, but the base data is free from USITC and already wrapped by a free tool (Wove), at least two dedicated APIs and six-plus cheap Apify actors. I did not research insurance or regulation in this pass.

### Cited Findings
- USITC HTS data is free and queryable; paid value is stable schema, change detection, historical rates and 232/301/IEEPA overlays — [dev.to guide](https://dev.to/rmccharles/how-to-get-us-and-canadian-tariff-data-via-api-the-complete-developer-guide-1o71) (written by a vendor).
- Dedicated APIs: TradeFacts.io (US and Canada, nightly from USITC/CBSA, 30-day trial) — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/posts/tradefacts-io-us-canada-tariff-api); TariffsAPI (checkout/e-commerce, historical `as_of`) — [TariffsAPI](https://tariffsapi.com/).
- Apify actors wrapping USITC from $2 per 1,000 results, some with 232/301/IEEPA overlays — [Apify](https://apify.com/jungle_synthesizer/usitc-hts-tariff-classification-scraper); [Apify](https://apify.com/pink_comic/us-tariff-hts-lookup).
- Wove offered a free tariff tool for forwarders and got API requests from importers — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/wove-speeds-tariff-change-info-for-forwarders-tms-and-its-free).

### Inferences
- Tariff lookup is commoditised. Any remaining gap is in decision-grade derived data (landed-cost by SKU, exclusion/refund eligibility), which shades into compliance software and liability rather than a data feed.
- Energy data demand overlaps section 4 (large-load interconnection, behind-the-meter generation) and is better pursued there.

### Gaps
- Insurance, regulatory-change and other community-specific needs were not researched (tool-call budget and blocked fetches). No findings; this is an open area, not a negative result.
- No revenue or customer counts for any tariff API.

## 7. Comparison of the most promising options for a solo founder

### Takeaway
Ranked by expected value under the stated constraints: (1) a narrow AI-infrastructure/power sub-niche dataset from public filings, (2) a fixed-panel vertical "AI recommendation share" benchmark sold to many buyers, then well behind, (3) a low-priced vertical per-brand AI-visibility tracker, (4) vertical data API/MCP for agents, (5) LLM market data, (6) training-data licensing. Options 1 and 2 are my inferences from the evidence above; neither was validated with buyers.

### Cited Findings
- Price anchors: infrastructure datasets $9,000/year plus $5,000 API — [Cleanview](https://cleanview.co/pricing); AI-visibility SaaS $19-$495/month — [Everything PR](https://everything-pr.com/profound-vs-peec-ai), [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/positionbird); agent micropayments mostly under one cent — [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/state-of-ai-agent-payments-2026/).
- Speed-to-revenue precedent: GEO tool MVP in two months, mid-five-figure MRR within a year (founder-reported) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/growing-a-geo-tool-to-a-mid-five-figure-mrr-within-a-year-FkhE79ioFu4EpEpJOXsA); against a $26 MRR solo counter-example — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brandpresence).
- Polling cost anchors: $0.01 to $0.30 per prompt-engine answer — [Detailed](https://detailed.com/ai-visibility-tools-pricing/) (attribution uncertain), [Apify actor](https://apify.com/doesaiknow/ai-brand-visibility-tracker-chatgpt-perplexity-gemini).

### Inferences
| Option | Buyers | Price point | Acquisition method and cost | Scarcity / defensibility | Competition | Time to first revenue (my estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Infrastructure/power sub-niche dataset (e.g. utility large-load tariffs, local data-centre permitting and opposition, neocloud list price/availability) | Developers, utilities, investors, equipment vendors | $2K-$10K/year per dataset | Scrape public filings; LLM extraction; under $200/month | Medium: normalisation plus history accumulates; sources are public | Whole-market taken (Cleanview, Interconnection.fyi, Yes Energy, Enverus); sub-niches unverified | 3-6 months; outbound B2B sales |
| B. Fixed-panel vertical AI-recommendation benchmark (one category, all engines, weekly) | Brands/vendors in one vertical, their agencies, investors | $100-$500/month or report sales | LLM APIs / per-answer providers; roughly $50-$200/month for a few thousand answers | Medium: longitudinal history cannot be backfilled by late entrants | Per-brand trackers are crowded; category panels not seen in results | 1-3 months if a vertical with reachable buyers is chosen |
| C. Low-priced vertical per-brand tracker (local, Shopify, etc.) | SMBs, agencies | $19-$79/month | Cost scales with customers; thin margins at honest sample sizes | Low | Very high; several bootstrapped entrants per niche | 1-2 months, but high churn/CAC risk |
| D. Vertical data API/MCP for agents | Agent builders | Cents per call or $19-$149/month | Depends on dataset | Only as strong as the dataset | Free MCP servers dominate | Unclear; no revenue precedent found |
| E. LLM price/usage tracker | AI teams, analysts | Low | Free public data | Low | Artificial Analysis | Poor |
| F. Training/eval licensing | About five labs plus brokers | Large, lumpy | Requires owned corpus or expert labour | High for owners | $100M+ vendors | Not realistic solo |
- Options A and B share a property worth weighting: one collection run serves all customers, and the time series is the moat. Options C and D do not have it.
- Overall answer to the objective: AI-driven demand does contain plausible solo niches, but they are in (d) and a reframed version of (c). Categories (a) and (b) are not realistic as primary businesses on the evidence found.

### Gaps
- No buyer validation for options A or B; the specific sub-niches are hypotheses.
- Time-to-revenue figures are my estimates, not sourced.
- Whether non-AI niches researched by other researchers beat these is outside this scope.
