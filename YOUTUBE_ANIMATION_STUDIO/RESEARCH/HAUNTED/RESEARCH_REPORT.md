## 2. What the evidence says about demand
1. **Named famous locations outperform generic ghosts.** The three Sam and Colby outliers all name a location with a pop-culture anchor: *The Shining* (Stanley), *The Conjuring* (the house), and the famous liner (Queen Mary). BuzzFeed made "Return to…" episodes for the Winchester and Sallie houses. **Implication:** lead with the place name plus its pop-culture hook.
2. **"TRUE" is the dominant genre word.** It recurs across MrBallen, Mr. Nightmare, Top5s and Corpse Husband titles. The audience pays for *claimed* reality.
3. **Skeptic framing is rising in 2026.** Recent Slapped Ham titles: "So Real Even a **Cop** Can't Debunk Them", "Made Even The **Skeptics** Go Quiet". BuzzFeed's believer/skeptic duo is the long-form version of this. Viewers want permission to believe from someone who doubts.
4. **Animation works for "true" horror.** Llama Arts (22M), the reported MrBallen animated outlier, and ParaHaunt's 2025–26 growth all show that stylised animation does not kill the "it's real" feeling, as long as the narration signals real sources.
5. **The horror-narration giants barely use Shorts** (Mr. Nightmare ~1%). MrBallen is the exception (~32%) because his stories have a single twist that fits in 60 s. **Opportunity:** a dedicated, animated, fact-labelled haunted-location Shorts channel faces less direct competition from the big narrators than long-form does (hypothesis).

## 3. Anatomy of a successful haunted video
### First 2 / 5 / 15 seconds
- **0–2 s:** the location named or shown *with one wrong detail already in frame*: a door ajar, a figure in a photo, a tapped wall. The spoken line is a factual-sounding anomaly, not "Hey guys". Example: *"In 1936 a magazine photographer pointed his camera up this staircase, and something was coming down it."*
- **2–5 s:** the credibility anchor: a date, a named witness role ("a police officer", "the keeper of the Crown Jewels") or a physical artefact ("the tape still exists").
- **5–15 s:** stakes and the question the video will answer ("what was on the recording?"), plus one escalation (a second witness, a second incident).

### Slow burn vs jump reveal
- Long-form hits (Sam and Colby, BuzzFeed) **slow-burn**: 20–40 minutes of build for a few seconds of anomaly.
- In Shorts there is no time to slow-burn, so use **micro slow-burn**: stillness and silence from about 30 to 45 s, then the reveal at roughly 70–80% of runtime. **Avoid full-screen jump scares with loud stingers**: they spike swipe-away and annoy a feed audience. Prefer a *quiet* reveal (something already in the frame that the viewer only now notices). This also suits the pipeline: a static camera plus one detail change is cheap to render.

### Sound design (half the effect)
- Room tone and low drone under the narration; cut the music to **silence** just before the reveal (the "dead air" beat).
- Diegetic, identifiable sounds beat generic stingers: knocks (Enfield), a door clunk (Queen Mary Door 13), tyres and a thump (Blue Bell Hill), a camera shutter (Brown Lady, Bachelor's Grove).
- Where a real recording exists (Enfield "voice"), **do not reproduce it.** Describe it, and render a waveform graphic. This avoids copyright problems and any implication of fabricated audio.

### "It's real" framing, and how hits signal fact vs claim
- Successful creators use **attribution verbs**: "reported", "she told investigators", "according to the Society for Psychical Research". The best performers keep their credibility by flagging the doubt themselves (BuzzFeed's Shane, Slapped Ham's "debunk" titles).
- On-screen **source cards** (newspaper headline, year, archive name) are the visual shorthand for fact.
- **Our rule (from the brief):** every claim carries a label. Proposed on-screen system: a small corner tag that changes colour.
  - `DOCUMENTED`: white tag, serif font, with the citation year.
  - `WITNESS CLAIM`: amber tag, "reported by …".
  - `FOLKLORE`: grey-green tag, "local legend".
  - `DRAMATISATION`: red outline tag, "recreation".
  The labels become a recognisable brand device as well as an ethics requirement.

### Endings: unresolved chill plus loop
- Hits end **unresolved**: no proof, no full debunk. The last line hands the anomaly to the viewer ("She's still reported on that road, so if you drive Archer Avenue at night and someone asks for a lift…").
- **Loop:** end on the same frame the video opened with, but with the wrong detail changed (the figure is closer; the door is now open). The final narration line should grammatically lead into the first ("…and the photographer never saw her" → "In 1936 a magazine photographer…").

### Thumbnails (for Shorts: the cover frame and the long-form cut)
- A dark, mostly empty scene, **one** light source and **one wrong detail**: a silhouette on the stairs, a handprint, a door open a crack. A red circle or arrow is optional and dated.
- 2–3 words of text at most, often the place or the number ("ROOM 217", "DOOR 13").

### Titles and curiosity gaps
Templates seen or derived from evidence:
- "The [Place] Photo No One Can Explain" (one artefact)
- "Why [Authority] Bans Entry After Sunset" (Bhangarh; official-rule hook)
- "The Room That Inspired *The Shining*" (pop-culture anchor)
- "Even the Skeptics Can't Explain [X]" (skeptic framing, 2026 trend)
- Use "TRUE" sparingly, and only where something is documented.

### Emotional triggers
Dread of the ordinary made wrong (a bedroom, a road, a staircase); the "this could be my house or my drive" transfer; a respectable witness; the forbidden place (night bans, closed islands); an unfinished story.

### What separates hits from the median
1. A **famous named place** with a pop-culture or historical anchor.
2. **A single concrete artefact**: a photo, a tape or a sign. Not "people feel cold spots".
3. **Credibility tension**: a skeptic or authority voice.
4. **Visual payoff**: something to *see* in the reveal.
5. Respect: the hoax-heavy channels earn views but also debunking backlash (Wingate Room 209 was staged; Chills was fooled by a planted fake). Honest labelling protects the channel long-term.

## 4. Ethics notes for this niche
- Avoid Aokigahara and the Lemp Mansion (suicide-centred stories; YouTube's self-harm policy and the monetisation risk). Avoid Hinterkaifeck and Villisca (murder) and Amityville (murder plus hoax).
- Where a ghost is tied to a real death (Blue Bell Hill 1965, Queen Mary Door 13): **do not name the deceased on screen**, no crash or injury imagery, and frame it as "reports attached to a tragedy".
- Myrtles Plantation: the "Chloe" ghost is an invented story about an enslaved woman. Only tell it as *the invention of a legend*, and centre the documented history respectfully.
- No real ghost photos in frame. Recreate scenes stylistically; describe the original photos and cite where they were published.

## 5. FORMAT TEMPLATE: HAUNTED SHORT (~60 s, 9:16)
| Time | Beat | Picture | Sound | Label |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0–2.0 | **Cold-open anomaly** | Static wide of the location (night, fog). The ONE wrong detail is already visible but small | Room tone; a single diegetic sound (knock, shutter, door clunk) | none, or `DOCUMENTED` if the line is a fact |
| 2.0–6.0 | **Anchor** | Push-in; on-screen card "[PLACE], [YEAR]" | Low drone enters | `DOCUMENTED` |
| 6.0–15.0 | **Stakes** | Map or timeline motion graphic → back to the location | Drone builds | `DOCUMENTED` / `WITNESS CLAIM` |
| 15–30 | **Witness account** | Silhouette character (no face) re-enacts; the camera follows the claim | Narration close-mic; diegetic SFX | `WITNESS CLAIM` + `DRAMATISATION` tag |
| 30–40 | **Second incident / escalation** | New angle on the same space; flashlight cone; the wrong detail has moved | Music thins out | `WITNESS CLAIM` or `FOLKLORE` |
| 40–45 | **Skeptic beat** | Evidence-board card: "What skeptics say" | Near-silence | `DOCUMENTED` (the counter-evidence) |
| 45–52 | **Dead-air reveal** | Slow push to the detail the viewer missed; it is now clearly wrong | **Silence**, then one sound | `DRAMATISATION` |
| 52–58 | **Unresolved hand-off** | Return toward the opening composition | Drone returns quietly | none |
| 58–60 | **Loop frame** | Same as frame 0, with the detail changed; the last line flows into the first | Same SFX as 0.0 s | none |

Captions: word-synced, burned in, 2–4 words per line, lower-middle third (clear of Shorts UI).
Voice: Kokoro `bm_george` (British locations) or `am_onyx` (US); slow pace, about 140 wpm (~140 words for 60 s).
Ending CTA: none spoken. The loop is the CTA.
