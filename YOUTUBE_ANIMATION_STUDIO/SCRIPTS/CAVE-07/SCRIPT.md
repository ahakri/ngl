## Clean read (for TTS / SRT)

Two divers dropped into this hole, chasing 1,000 feet.
Only one came back up.
Zacatón, Mexico. April 1994. Nobody knew how deep it went.
One diver was Jim Bowden.
The other was Sheck Exley, a legend of cave diving.
The first person ever to log 1,000 cave dives.
His cave-diving safety manual was titled Basic Cave Diving: A Blueprint for Survival.
They went down on separate lines, each alone in the dark.
Around 925 feet, Bowden was burning gas faster than planned.
He turned back, and set a world depth record.
Exley's light never came back up.
Three days later, his team hauled up his line.
Exley came up with it, wrapped in the line.
The cause of his death was never determined.
He was 45.
Today, cave divers can earn an award in his name.
It's for logging 1,000 safe cave dives.
