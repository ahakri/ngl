## Clean read (for TTS / SRT)

Who put Bella down the Wych Elm?
In 1944, someone wrote that on a wall in Birmingham.
It was about a real woman. Nobody knows who she was.
April 1943. Four boys were out in Hagley Wood, in Worcestershire.
One climbed an old wych elm and looked down into its hollow trunk.
In the dark, he saw a skull.
Inside the tree, police found the skeleton of a woman.
Investigators estimated she'd been dead about a year and a half.
She was probably about 35, and five feet tall.
There was a shoe, a wedding ring, and scraps of clothing.
But no one could put a name to her.
Then, in 1944, the messages began, calling her Bella.
The writer of the messages was never found.
In 1945, an anthropologist suggested witchcraft.
Investigators dismissed the idea.
A 1953 letter to a local paper tied her to a wartime spy ring.
That claim was never proven either.
And today, even her skeleton is missing.
So are key case records.
For decades, the question has kept reappearing on an obelisk near the wood.
More than 80 years on, all that's left is the question.

**Loop line:** More than 80 years on, all that's left is the question.
