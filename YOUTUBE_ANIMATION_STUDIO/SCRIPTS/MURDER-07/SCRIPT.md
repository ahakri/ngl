## Clean read (for TTS / SRT)

This cold case is 5,300 years old.
September 1991. Two German hikers spot a body melting out of a glacier.
High in the Ötztal Alps, on the border of Italy and Austria.
At first, everyone assumed it was a climber who'd died recently.
Then radiocarbon dating put his life around 3300 BC.
Europe's oldest known natural human mummy. They named him Ötzi.
He carried a copper axe, a flint dagger and an unfinished bow.
But for ten years, nobody knew how he died.
In 2001, a new X-ray caught something in his left shoulder.
A flint arrowhead.
He'd been shot from behind.
Looking back, it was visible on an X-ray from 1991.
In 2007, new CT scans looked closer.
The arrow had torn a major artery under his collarbone.
Researchers concluded he most likely bled to death.
Shortly before, he'd eaten a hearty meal, including ibex.
Who shot him, and why, is still unknown.
Today, he's kept on ice in a museum in Bolzano.

**Loop line:** Today, he's kept on ice in a museum in Bolzano.
