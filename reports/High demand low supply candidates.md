# High-demand, low-supply candidates: result of the red-team selection

27 November 2026. Ten candidate data businesses were each given a quantified case (buyers, price anchors, every competitor found, revenue arithmetic), scored 1–10 against four tests, then handed to an independent red team told to try to kill them. Figures rest on web-search summaries; pages could not be opened. Three red-team passes (insurer payment rates, grant prospecting first pass, workers' comp) could not run fresh searches and worked from the case alone.

**Result: all ten were killed. None meets the bar.** Every one fails on supply: small self-serve competitors or free tools already exist at the prices a solo product would charge.

Scores shown are the red team's corrected scores.

| Candidate | Demand | Monetisation | Scarcity | Solo feasibility | Why it was killed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grant prospecting from IRS 990 data | 6 | 3 | 2 | 7 | At least ten sellers at $0–99/month, including free Grantmakers.io and Zeffy |
| Insurer payment-rate benchmarks for small practices | 5 | 3 | 2 | 5 | Eleven direct competitors, free to $99/month |
| Planning-stage construction leads | 5 | 3 | 2 | 5 | Free search (Hamlet, GatherGov) plus alerts from $29/month |
| Keyword alerts across local governments | 5 | 3 | 2 | 5 | A layer of free products (DocketCity, OpenTowns, Hamlet) |
| AI-recommendation benchmark (law firms) | 5 | 3 | 2 | 6 | The exact product exists, covering ~1,500 firms in 256 markets |
| Franchise disclosure data | 5 | 3 | 1 | 6 | Saturated; a dozen more products found, several free with APIs |
| Healthcare sales prospecting from federal data | 4 | 3 | 1 | 7 | Funded incumbents now give this layer away free |
| Home and auto insurance rate filings | 4 | 3 | 2 | 3 | Three funded products own the wedge; the main source forbids scripted collection |
| Wildcard: commercial property loans coming due | 4 | 2 | 1 | 7 | Free tools on the same SEC source; paid rival from $97 |
| Wildcard: workers' comp renewal dates | 5 | 3 | 3 | 3 | A self-serve incumbent already covers 28+ states |

Scales: demand 7 = tens of thousands of buyers; monetisation 5 = about $100k a year within 24 months, 3 = roughly $30–50k; scarcity 3–4 = several affordable competitors exist, 7 = enterprise-only.

## What this means

- Markets with many buyers and public source data attract small entrants quickly; many of the competitors found launched in 2024–2026, often as free tools.
- The realistic outcome for a late solo entrant in any of these is tens of thousands of dollars a year, not hundreds of thousands.
- The data-centre siting tracker already built (Siteline) has narrower demand but the least cheap direct competition of anything examined.

## Implication

A data product that is high on demand, monetisation and scarcity at once, and buildable by one person from public sources, was not found in ten attempts. What separates outcomes is who can reach the buyers, not which niche is chosen. See `Student-feasible options.md` for what follows from that.
