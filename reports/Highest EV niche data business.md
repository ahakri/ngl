# Build the data-centre siting tracker

The highest expected-value niche in the research is a **project-level tracker of US data-centre siting records**: local zoning and moratorium decisions, state air permits for on-site power, and utility large-load dockets, joined into one alerting dataset. It is the only candidate where three of the five research streams independently converged, where a small team already sells a neighbouring dataset at a public price of **$9,000 a year**, and where every source is a government record with no personal data. The runner-up is **state insurance rate-filing intelligence for one line of business**, which has more durable demand but slower buyers and an unresolved terms-of-use question. The recommended product sells at roughly $400 a month self-serve to developers, site-selection consultants, equipment vendors, law firms and infrastructure investors, with first revenue plausible in three to six months. That price and timeline are estimates anchored on competitor prices; nobody interviewed a buyer. The margin over the runner-up is a judgement call, not a measured gap. All of the evidence comes from search-result summaries, because the researchers could not open primary pages, so the first week of work should be checking the two closest competitors' actual coverage before writing any code.

## Proven solo data businesses share one shape

The cases with credible revenue all structure public-but-messy data, keep it fresh, and sell it to businesses that make money from it. Recent solo launches with founder-stated numbers sit between $1.5K and $20K a month. Scrape Creators reached **$10K+ MRR in about 12 months** ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/6iF8SJRF4WpciDff9aYi)), while a generic funded-startup lead list plateaued at about $1.5K a month with 70% of the founder's time spent on upkeep ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/vcbacked)). The difference is whether a direct substitute exists.

The failures are equally consistent. Proxycurl reached roughly $10M ARR and then shut down in July 2025 after LinkedIn sued over fake accounts and scraped member profiles ([founder post](https://nubela.co/blog/goodbye-proxycurl/)). Podscan lost profitability when one large customer churned ([The Bootstrapped Founder](https://thebootstrappedfounder.com/when-profitability-disappears-a-podscan-reality-check/)). The lesson for niche selection is to draw on many small official sources, not one litigious platform, and to sell to many mid-sized buyers.

Scraping law points the same way. Logged-out collection of public facts remains broadly defensible in the US, but in July 2026 a New York federal court let Reddit's anti-circumvention claims proceed, holding that CAPTCHAs and bot detection can count as access controls ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/reddit-v-serpapi-llc)). Niches that depend on bot-defended sites (apartment listings, big-retailer prices) now carry real legal exposure. Government filings do not.

Two tempting channels fail on the evidence. Selling to hedge funds pays well, with most subscriptions in the **$25k to $150k a year** band ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2bsx5x6m2pew8jzgavmyo/corner-office/how-much-are-managers-paying-for-data)), but validation alone runs three to six months ([Selling Signals podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2567619/episodes/19540236-kai-searle-hiring-elite-data-sellers)) and funds reject most datasets over missing point-in-time history ([Monda](https://www.monda.ai/blog/selling-data-to-hedge-funds-why-alternative-data-trials-fail-and-how-to-fix-them)). Funds are a second-stage upsell. Selling to AI agents is not yet a business at all: 6.4 million x402 payments tracked in mid-2026 were worth under $120,000 in total ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/state-of-ai-agent-payments-2026/)).

## Most candidate niches fail on scarcity or buyer speed

The researchers screened roughly thirty verticals. Most drop out quickly. Job postings, sports odds, Amazon pricing, building permits, procurement, bills of lading and licence lead feeds are saturated, often with per-record scrapers selling at a few dollars per thousand rows ([Apify](https://apify.com/wallman_3rd/liquor-license-monitor)). Tariff data is free from USITC and already wrapped by several cheap tools ([Apify](https://apify.com/jungle_synthesizer/usitc-hts-tariff-classification-scraper)). Per-brand AI-visibility tracking has proven revenue but is crowded from $19 a month to enterprise, and one solo entrant shows **$26 MRR** on a verified listing ([TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/brandpresence)). Training-data licensing flows to owners of large archives and to expert-labour vendors, neither of which a solo founder has.

Six candidates survive that screen. The table compares them; price and time-to-revenue cells are the researchers' estimates anchored on competitor prices, not observed sales.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Data-centre siting records** (zoning, moratoria, air permits, large-load dockets) | Strong and fresh: 127 moratoria, ~$130B of projects stalled | High: no consolidated source; free trackers are advocacy lists | Public agency portals; LLM extraction; low legal risk | Cleanview, datacenter.fyi, Halcyon nearby; none found doing the cross-source join cheaply | $200 to $750/mo, under a $9k/yr anchor | 3 to 6 months |

The data-centre and insurance niches lead because both have a visible gap between free regulator documents and five-figure enterprise contracts. The AI-recommendation benchmark is the fastest to launch but has no evidence that anyone pays for category-level data. GLP-1 pricing is easy but its market is contracting: one roundup counts only eight major providers still offering compounded semaglutide ([Telehealth Ally](https://www.telehealthally.com/compare/compounded-semaglutide-providers-roundup)).

## The data-centre buildout made three sleepy record types valuable

Demand here comes from a measurable shock. A tracker counted **127 local jurisdictions with data-centre moratoriums** by August 2026 ([Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/artificial-intelligence/2026/08/many-localities-have-tried-data-center-moratoriums-tracker-finds/415696/)). At least 75 projects worth about $130B were blocked or delayed in the first quarter of 2026 ([Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/4200222/data-center-energy-constraints-and-moratoriums-are-mounting-expect-to-see-stalled-ai-projects.html)). State regulators approved 29 large-load tariffs in 2025 ([EPRI](https://restservice.epri.com/publicattachment/98541)). Developers have announced up to 138 GW of on-site gas generation, and reporters found thousands of on-site power sources approved through minor permits with no public notice ([Sierra Club](https://www.sierraclub.org/sites/default/files/2026-11/artificialintegrity_report_pdf.pdf)). Those permits are visible only inside individual state agency systems.

Supply is thin where it matters. The free trackers are association and advocacy products, not structured feeds. Cleanview sells a US data-centre dataset and a separate grid-power dataset at **$9,000 a year each**, with a $5,000 API add-on ([Cleanview](https://cleanview.co/pricing)). Interconnection.fyi sells queue data by quote and forbids scraping its site ([Interconnection.fyi terms](https://www.interconnection.fyi/terms)). Shovels' meeting-decisions product covered about 100 cities when acquired in January 2026 ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/7ee041bd-8e7d-45d8-8815-ff1d24000c5c/)). A search found no national dataset consolidating large-load requests; ERCOT publishes its large-load queue only inside committee report PDFs ([ERCOT](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/03/12/March-TAC-Report.pdf)).

Collection fits one person. An INFORMS tutorial converted meeting PDFs to structured JSON at about **$0.0018 per page** ([INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/full/10.1287/inte.2026.0314)), so 100,000 pages a month costs a few hundred dollars. One collection run serves every customer, and the accumulated history cannot be backfilled by a later entrant.

### The concrete product

The product is a **Data-Centre Siting Risk Tracker**. Each row is one project or site, linked to its zoning or conditional-use case, any moratorium or tax-abatement vote, its air-permit application for on-site generation, and the relevant large-load tariff or docket. Each record carries status, dates, capacity, parties and a link to the source document.

Sources are county and municipal agenda and minutes portals, state environmental-agency permit systems such as TCEQ, state utility-commission e-filing systems, and grid-operator reports. Coverage starts in the highest-activity states (Virginia, Georgia, Texas, Arizona, Ohio), where a few hundred jurisdictions account for most activity. Collection must come from these primary sources, never from competitors' sites.

Buyers are data-centre developers, site-selection consultants and land brokers first, then turbine and engine makers, engineering contractors, land-use and energy law firms, utilities, and infrastructure investors. Pricing has two tiers, both my estimates: **about $400 a month** for alerts and the dataset, and **about $9,000 a year** for API access and full history, matching Cleanview's anchor. Twenty self-serve customers would yield roughly $96K a year. A free public map of moratoria serves as the lead magnet, as Cleanview's free map does.

## Insurance rate filings are the runner-up

Insurance filings beat the data-centre niche on durability, since carriers reprice regardless of any boom. Regulators already publish usable reports: Oklahoma posts monthly homeowners and auto rate-change reports by company ([Oklahoma Insurance Department](https://www.oid.ok.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/HO-Jan-2026-Aug-2026-SERFF-State-Rate_change-Report.pdf)), and Texas offers an exportable dataset ([Texas Department of Insurance](https://tdi.texas.gov/consumer/home-and-auto-insurance-rate-filings.html)). Incumbents are all sales-led, and one targets only the top 25 carriers ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/insuraviews/discuss)).

It ranks second for three reasons. The legal position is unresolved: the SERFF licence bars interfering with its operations, and nobody read the public Filing Access terms ([SERFF licence agreement](https://serff.com/documents/implementation_standard_serff_license_agreement.pdf)). The valuable detail sits in dense actuarial exhibits, where LLM accuracy on multi-page tables is known to be weak ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10659)). And the buyers are insurers, a regulated segment the buyer research rated among the slowest. If the data-centre check in week one shows the niche is already covered, this is the fallback, starting with the states that publish structured reports.

## Four risks could sink the recommendation

**Incumbent overlap is the largest unknown.** Cleanview already sells a behind-the-meter tracker that follows each on-site power project "from its first permit" ([Cleanview](https://cleanview.co/products/behind-the-meter-tracker)), and Halcyon, with about $31.8M raised, indexes all 50 utility commissions and offers a free app ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260316032633/en/Halcyon-Raises-%2421-Million-Series-A-To-Bring-AI-Powered-Intelligence-to-the-Energy-Industry)). The researchers could not open either site. If they cover local approvals well, the open space shrinks to local zoning and opposition records alone.

**Demand is tied to one investment cycle.** A slowdown in data-centre construction cuts the buyer pool directly. Policy churn also removes records: a January 2026 EPA rule reduced permit obligations for some turbines ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/epa-s-new-turbine-rules-provide-air-9433112/)).

**Willingness to pay is unmeasured.** No interviews, job postings or tenders were gathered for any candidate. Every price in this report is anchored on a competitor's list price.

**Maintenance and accuracy are untested.** No source measured scraper breakage across hundreds of local portals, or LLM accuracy on air-permit applications. Per-state commercial-use terms for permit portals were not checked.

The evidence base itself is weak in a specific way. Every finding came from search-result summaries. Revenue figures for comparables are mostly third-party estimates; Cleanview's roughly $342K revenue is a low-confidence model ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/c/cleanview-revenue)). "Underserved" means only that searches found no cheap, narrow product, which is absence of evidence. The $9,000 Cleanview price is the firmest anchor, cited by three research streams, but still unread first-hand.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which community has data" to "which fragmented public record just became expensive to ignore". Cheap LLM extraction has commoditised any single-source feed, so the scarce asset is now the join across sources and the timestamped history behind it. That favours starting collection immediately, even before the first sale, because history is the one thing a later, better-funded entrant cannot buy.

The decision should stay reversible for one week. Read Cleanview's and datacenter.fyi's product pages directly, then ask five developers or site-selection consultants whether they would pay $400 a month for local-approval alerts. If either check fails, the insurance rate-filing product uses the same pipeline (agency portals, PDF extraction, change alerts) and the switch costs little.
